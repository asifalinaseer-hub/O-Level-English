Core Notes

What Directed Writing Really Tests

Directed writing tests whether students can transform information into a suitable written form for a specific audience and purpose.

🎯

Format

  • Use the correct form: email, letter, report, article or speech.
  • Show conventions without overdecorating.
  • Do not use the same layout for every task.
🧠

Audience

  • Know who you are addressing.
  • Choose formality and vocabulary accordingly.
  • Avoid slang in formal tasks.
✍️

Purpose

  • Inform, persuade, complain, advise, recommend or explain.
  • Use content that serves the purpose.
  • Do not drift into unrelated storytelling.
CIE-Aligned Skill Map

Main Directed Writing Forms

Each form has its own structure and tone.

Email

  • Subject or clear opening.
  • Usually direct and purposeful.

Formal Letter

  • Address-like tone and formal register.
  • Clear reason for writing.

Informal Letter

  • Warm tone and personal voice.
  • Still organised and accurate.

Report

  • Headings and factual organisation.
  • Objective style and recommendations.

Article

  • Headline and engaging opening.
  • Audience-friendly style.

Speech

  • Direct address to audience.
  • Rhetorical devices and clear ending.

Register

  • Formal, semi-formal or informal.
  • Maintain consistency.

Content Use

  • Use all relevant task details.
  • Do not copy mechanically.
A* Method

The Directed Writing Method

Decode the task before writing.

1

Identify the Form

  • What exactly are you writing?
  • Use correct layout and conventions.
2

Identify Audience

  • Teacher, friend, public, committee, reader or community?
  • Choose register accordingly.
3

Identify Purpose

  • Are you informing, persuading, advising, complaining or recommending?
  • Every paragraph must serve this purpose.
4

Select Relevant Content

  • Use task points carefully.
  • Add development, not irrelevant material.
5

Organise Paragraphs

  • Opening, development and ending.
  • Use headings where appropriate.
6

Proofread Tone and Accuracy

  • Check politeness, clarity and grammar.
  • Make sure the format is visible.
Answer Formula

F-A-P-R Formula

This formula prevents common directed writing mistakes.

F

Format

  • Show the required form clearly.
  • Do not confuse article, report and speech.
A

Audience

  • Adapt vocabulary and tone to the reader.
  • Respect relationship and context.
P

Purpose

  • Know the reason for writing.
  • Keep the response task-focused.
R

Register

  • Formal, semi-formal or informal.
  • Do not mix registers carelessly.

Practice Plan

Practise Directed Writing by Form

Rotate forms weekly so students become flexible.

Weekly Directed Writing Practice

Focus on one form at a time.

Day 1 Write one formal email.
Day 2 Write one report with headings.
Day 3 Write one article opening and conclusion.
Day 4 Write one speech using direct address.
Day 5 Rewrite one task in a different register.
Day 6 Complete one timed directed writing response.
Before writing, say aloud: I am writing as whom, to whom, for what purpose, and in what tone?
