Core Notes

What Narrative Writing Really Tests

Narrative writing is not just a sequence of events. It tests whether students can shape a meaningful story with control.

🎯

Plot

  • Something must change from beginning to end.
  • Events need cause and consequence.
  • Avoid random incidents.
🧠

Character

  • Characters need motives, fears or desires.
  • Show personality through action and dialogue.
  • Do not introduce too many characters.
✍️

Conflict

  • Conflict creates interest.
  • It may be internal, external or situational.
  • A story without conflict feels flat.
CIE-Aligned Skill Map

Narrative Writing Toolkit

Use these elements to make stories engaging.

Hook

  • Begin with action, tension or mystery.
  • Avoid slow weather openings.

Setting

  • Place the reader quickly.
  • Use details that affect the story.

Character Goal

  • What does the character want?
  • Why does it matter?

Conflict

  • What blocks the goal?
  • Raise stakes gradually.

Dialogue

  • Use dialogue to reveal tension.
  • Do not overuse casual conversation.

Pacing

  • Slow down important moments.
  • Speed through minor actions.

Climax

  • Build to a decisive moment.
  • Do not make the climax accidental.

Ending

  • Resolve or leave controlled ambiguity.
  • Avoid 'it was all a dream'.
A* Method

The Narrative Planning Method

A strong story needs shape before language.

1

Start Close to the Problem

  • Begin near conflict, not far before it.
  • Make the reader curious early.
2

Give the Character a Goal

  • The goal may be escape, truth, apology, victory or survival.
  • The goal creates direction.
3

Create Obstacles

  • Add difficulty and pressure.
  • Make events connected.
4

Build Tension

  • Reveal information gradually.
  • Use pacing and short sentences.
5

Reach a Climax

  • Make the main problem peak.
  • Let the character make a meaningful choice.
6

End with Control

  • Resolve the key conflict.
  • Leave a final image, thought or consequence.
Answer Formula

H-C-C-E Formula

Use this structure to avoid weak storytelling.

H

Hook

  • Open with a moment that demands attention.
  • Use action, mystery or emotion.
C

Character

  • Give the main character motive and personality.
  • Show feelings through behaviour.
C

Conflict

  • Create a problem that drives the story.
  • Escalate the problem.
E

Ending

  • Resolve the story with impact.
  • Avoid rushed or fake endings.

Basic plot

Controlled plot

Flat characters

Some personality

Clear ending

Impactful ending

Practice Plan

Practise Narrative Craft

Good stories come from planning and rewriting.

Weekly Narrative Practice

Train one story skill at a time.

Day 1 Write five different opening hooks.
Day 2 Plan three character goals and obstacles.
Day 3 Write one dialogue scene with tension.
Day 4 Practise slowing down a climax.
Day 5 Write one complete story plan.
Day 6 Rewrite an ending to make it stronger.
A good story is not about how many events happen; it is about how strongly one problem develops and changes the character.
