Reading Foundations

Core Reading Skills Students Need

Reading marks improve when students learn the skill behind each question type.

📘

Inference

Understand what is suggested, not only stated.

  • Use clues from action and tone.
  • Avoid unsupported guesses.
  • Explain the logic behind the answer.
✍️

Evidence Selection

Choose the most useful proof from the passage.

  • Use short details.
  • Avoid whole-line copying.
  • Match evidence to the question.
🎯

Own Words

Paraphrase meaning accurately.

  • Change key words, not just small words.
  • Keep original meaning unchanged.
  • Use simple accurate vocabulary.
✨

Vocabulary in Context

Work out meaning from surrounding words.

  • Read before and after the word.
  • Notice tone and situation.
  • Avoid dictionary-only guessing.
🧠

Command Words

Know what identify, explain, why and how require.

  • Different command words need different answers.
  • Underline the task word.
  • Answer exactly what is asked.
📌

Summary Selection

Separate main points from examples.

  • Ignore repetition.
  • Select only relevant content.
  • Condense without losing meaning.

Skill Training Method

How to Improve Reading Step by Step

Reading improvement should be targeted, not random.

1

Diagnose Weakness

Check whether the student loses marks in inference, selection, vocabulary or explanation.

2

Practise One Skill

Do not practise all question types at once in the beginning.

3

Compare Answers

Show weak, average and A* answers side by side.

4

Rewrite Responses

Improve weak answers by adding precision and evidence.

5

Track Progress

Keep a list of repeated mistakes and fix them weekly.

Student Guidance

Reading Problems and Solutions

Every reading weakness has a teachable solution.

Common Problems

  • Cannot infer hidden meaning.
  • Copies too much from passage.
  • Misunderstands command words.
  • Uses vague vocabulary.
  • Writes too much in short answers.

Solutions

  • Practise clue-based inference.
  • Use short quotations only.
  • Learn command word meanings.
  • Build attitude vocabulary.
  • Answer in one or two focused sentences.

Teacher Tip

Reading skills become strong when students stop asking 'Where is the answer?' and start asking 'Which clue proves the answer?'
